Digitizing as-flown airframes to measure what flight hours actually do to them

A full-component 3D metrology and computational analysis framework for legacy aircraft — capturing real, weathered airframe geometry with high-resolution optical scanning, rebuilding it in SolidWorks as a digital twin, and quantifying its true aerodynamic penalties with CFD.

The Problem

When the CAD files don't exist anymore, neither does the truth about the airframe

Legacy and aging aircraft frequently operate where original tooling, replacement parts, and native CAD files simply don't exist. These airframes rely on compound-curved aerodynamic surfaces that are difficult to measure accurately with traditional hand tools without stacking up dimensional error. Over hundreds or thousands of flight hours, airframes accumulate skin waviness, fastener head wear, paint erosion, and localized surface damage. Because operators lack accurate digital models of the true "as-flown" hardware, it's tough to quantify how much these surfaces have drifted from nominal specs, verify symmetry across the airframe, or model real-world aerodynamic performance.

To close that gap, this project establishes an on-aircraft 3D metrology and reverse-engineering framework. High-resolution optical 3D scanning captures dense point clouds of critical aerodynamic contours and localized damage zones on an operational aircraft in Starkville. That scan data is processed and imported into SolidWorks to build both a nominal reference model and an as-flown digital twin.

A geometric deviation study compares mirrored symmetric counterparts — port vs. starboard wings or horizontal stabilizers — to measure physical airframe drift and asymmetry caused by flight hours. Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) is then run on both the nominal and scanned geometries to evaluate the real aerodynamic penalties of these imperfections: boundary layer transitions, surface pressure changes, and section drag increases.

Why it's hard to answer today

  • No native CAD or original tooling exists for most legacy airframes in service today.
  • Compound-curved outer mold lines can't be measured accurately by hand without stacking dimensional error.
  • Skin waviness, fastener wear, and paint erosion accumulate silently over thousands of flight hours.
  • Without an as-flown model, there's no way to verify symmetry or model true aerodynamic performance.
Overall Project Objectives

Four technical objectives, from scan to CFD

To establish and validate the digital metrology and aerodynamic analysis framework, the project executes four core technical objectives.

01

Full-Component 3D Metrology

Deploy high-resolution optical 3D scanning on an operational aircraft in Starkville to capture compound-curved outer mold lines (OML) across critical flight surfaces, generating dense spatial point clouds of the true physical condition — skin waviness, fastener profiles, paint erosion, and localized damage.

02

Parametric CAD & Digital Twin Modeling

Bridge raw scan point clouds into SolidWorks to reconstruct accurate CAD geometry — an idealized nominal CAD model, and a faceted as-flown digital twin that directly preserves the scanned surface irregularities for downstream analysis.

03

Comparative Geometric Deviation Analysis

Generate full-field 3D deviation heatmaps to quantify physical discrepancies across the airframe, comparing mirrored symmetric counterparts and measuring localized damage against the nominal reference geometry.

3a — Bilateral Symmetry

Mirror and compare symmetric counterparts — e.g. port vs. starboard wings or horizontal stabilizers — to measure geometric drift accumulated over operational flight hours.

3b — Damage Profiling

Measure localized dents, repairs, or surface wear directly against the nominal reference geometry.

04

Aerodynamic Computational Analysis (CFD)

Run comparative CFD simulations evaluating the clean nominal baseline against the scanned as-flown geometry to quantify the real aerodynamic penalties of surface degradation.

Caden Detre
Metrology Lead & CAD/Structural Integration
Zachary, LA

Core Focus

Field 3D scanning execution, point-cloud post-processing, parametric SolidWorks reconstruction, and geometric deviation inspection.

Key Responsibilities

  • Coordinate on-site access to the aircraft in Starkville; manage target-tracking setup and optical 3D scanning passes.
  • Post-process raw scan data — mesh cleanup, noise filtering, coordinate-system alignment — and extract 2D cross-sectional spline profiles.
  • Build the parametric nominal CAD models and generate the as-flown faceted digital twin geometries.
  • Perform pre-analysis 3D surface deviation heatmaps to quantify bilateral asymmetry and localized skin damage depth.
  • Correlate physical scan deviations with underlying structural load paths alongside Mr. Wolz.
Cole Monahan
Computational Aerodynamics & CFD Lead
Columbus, MS

Core Focus

Computational fluid grid generation, CFD solver execution, flow-field analysis, and aerodynamic post-processing.

Key Responsibilities

  • Define computational fluid domain boundaries, farfield conditions, and grid resolution strategies for both nominal and as-flown geometries.
  • Configure boundary-layer inflation layers to capture near-wall velocity gradients, skin friction, and transition behavior around imperfections.
  • Set up and run steady-state Reynolds-Averaged Navier–Stokes simulations across relevant angles of attack and flight Mach numbers.
  • Post-process CFD solutions to quantify section drag deltas, lift curve slopes, pressure coefficient spikes, and separation bubbles.
  • Coordinate directly with Dr. Way on solver verification, turbulence model selection, and grid convergence studies.

Shared Technical & Management Contributions

  • Collaborate on the overall statement of work, research, and regular progress reports for faculty advisors.
  • Jointly analyze the final aero-structural correlation — mapping measured physical deviations to aerodynamic drag and trim penalties.
  • Co-author the final technical report and assemble the capstone poster presentation and deliverables.
